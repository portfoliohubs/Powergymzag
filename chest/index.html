Chest Training

Complete chest workout for upper body strength and aesthetics

Barbell Bench Press

Free_weights Intermediate Chest Push
The barbell bench press is the king of upper body exercises for building chest strength and mass. This compound movement targets the chest, shoulders, and triceps simultaneously. Execution Lie on a flat bench with your eyes under the bar Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width Unrack the bar and lower it to your mid-chest Press the bar back up to the starting position Keep your feet flat on the floor throughout Key Points Maintain a slight arch in your lower back Keep your shoulder blades retracted and depressed Control the descent and explode on the way up Don’t bounce the bar off your chest Common Mistakes Flaring elbows too wide (should be about 45 degrees) Lifting your hips off the bench Using partial range of motion Bouncing the weight off your chest Progression Start with light weight to master form, then gradually increase the weight.

Cable Fly

Cables Intermediate Chest Push
The cable fly provides constant tension throughout the entire range of motion, making it superior to dumbbell flies for muscle isolation and mind-muscle connection. Execution Set pulleys at chest height on a cable machine Grab the handles with palms facing forward Step forward and create tension on the cables Bring your hands together in front of your chest Squeeze your chest muscles at the peak contraction Slowly return to the starting position Key Points Keep a slight bend in your elbows throughout Focus on squeezing the chest muscles Control the negative phase (returning movement) Don’t let the weights stack between reps Common Mistakes Using too much body momentum Locking elbows straight Not getting a full stretch at the start Rushing through the movement Variations High cable fly (targets lower chest) Low cable fly (targets upper chest) Single-arm cable fly (unilateral focus)

Dumbbell Fly

Free_weights Beginner Chest Push
The dumbbell fly is an isolation exercise that targets the chest muscles, particularly the outer chest for width and shape. It’s excellent for chest definition and stretching the muscle fibers. Execution Lie on a flat bench holding dumbbells above your chest Keep a slight bend in your elbows throughout the movement Lower the dumbbells out to your sides in a wide arc Feel the stretch in your chest at the bottom Bring the dumbbells back together by squeezing your chest Key Points Maintain constant tension on the chest muscles Keep elbows slightly bent, not locked Focus on the mind-muscle connection Control the movement, don’t use momentum Common Mistakes Bending elbows too much (turns it into a press) Using too much weight and losing form Dropping the weights too quickly Not getting a good stretch at the bottom Variations Incline dumbbell fly (targets upper chest) Decline dumbbell fly (targets lower chest) Cable fly (constant tension throughout)

Machine Chest Press

Machines Beginner Chest Push
The machine chest press is an excellent exercise for beginners and those looking to isolate the chest muscles with minimal risk of injury. It provides a fixed path of movement for consistent form. Execution Adjust the seat height so handles are at chest level Sit with your back flat against the pad Grip the handles with palms facing down Press the handles forward until your arms are extended Slowly return to the starting position Key Points Keep your back flat against the pad Don’t lock your elbows at full extension Focus on squeezing your chest muscles Control the movement in both directions Common Mistakes Using momentum instead of muscle Not adjusting the seat properly Locking elbows at the top Arching your back off the pad Benefits Safe for beginners learning proper form Isolates chest muscles effectively Consistent movement pattern Reduced risk of injury compared to free weights Easy to adjust weight increments
